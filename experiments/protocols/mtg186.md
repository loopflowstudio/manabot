# MTG-186: one seed trained only on UR vs GW, compared with MTG-181, for $10

Jack Heart, 2026-10-25. Question: does training on one matchup, UR vs GW, beat
MTG-181's training on all ten Elemental decks on that matchup, and how much does
it lose (or transfer) across the other 98 grid cells? One seed, $10 inclusive of
training and evaluation, matched as closely as possible to MTG-181's two-seat
run, launched through Etude.

No earlier data answers it: every UR-vs-GW-only run (MTG-159, the seat
comparison, the two-seat ladder) trained on the w6 Allies/Lessons pack, whose
decklists differ, whose checkpoints current code cannot load, and which were
scored against the v1 heuristic.

## Recipe

`experiments/regimes/elemental-ur-gw-186-v1.json` is MTG-181's frozen
`elemental-two-seat-181-v1` with one intended change: **training sees only UR vs
GW**. The authored match is `elemental_ur` (seat 0) vs `elemental_gw` (seat 1)
with their Lesson pools, and `matchup_curriculum: cross-balanced` assigns half
of the 256 streams each ordered pair, so each deck is on the play in half the
streams. Both seats learn. There are no mirrors. Everything else is MTG-181's:
the 64-wide, two-layer, four-head scalar masked-mean policy, Adam, the damped
policy learning parameters, absolute-iteration LR/tau schedules, EMA .999, 256
streams, 64 transitions per stream per update, CUDA float32, one producer, and
game-error recovery (10% threshold, 100/100 window/warmup, 40,000 actions).

Compatibility changes, none chosen for effect:

- **Per-stream pairs instead of per-game sampling.** MTG-181's
  `elemental-uniform` samples an ordered pair at each native game root and the
  native sampler admits only the complete ten-deck roster. The existing
  `cross-balanced` strata fix each stream's ordered pair. Both give each ordered
  pair half the exposure in expectation; strata balance transitions, not games.
- **Update target 616**, MTG-181's longest seed, instead of an unreachable
  100,000 cut by the allocation deadline. Learning rates and tau follow absolute
  iterations, so the schedule over updates 0–616 is identical. Exports at
  updates 0, 200, 400 and 600 and at the endpoint.
- **Wall ceiling 7.5 hours** (27,000 s) instead of 400,000 s, because Etude
  requires the job lifetime to cover the regime's ceiling. It is a safety bound;
  MTG-181 reached 474–616 updates in 4.75 hours.
- **Launch through Etude** (`docs/remote-training.md`); the former Manabot
  remote system was removed in #362. Etude's stock image has no Rust, so
  `TrainingJob` now installs the pinned toolchain (1.98.1, as the retired
  bootstrap did) and runs the locked sync in the job's setup.

Seed **18601**; Etude cohort `mtg186`, job `mtg186-ur-gw`. A local CPU smoke of
this regime (16 streams, 3 updates) played 48 games, 24 per ordered pair, with
no game errors, and collected about 3,400 learner decisions per update. The job
setup was rehearsed locally in the pinned image (amd64 emulation, $0): the
toolchain install and locked sync built the engine in 307 s and imported
`torch 2.10.0+cu128` and `managym` w13.

## Evaluation

The frozen MTG-181 10×10 grid (`experiments/elemental_grid.py`, schedule
`ecc487e4…264`, seed 18190001): the model plays each Elemental deck against the
heuristic playing each Elemental deck, mirrors included, 50 games per cell,
5,000 games per checkpoint, the same deals and seats for every checkpoint. The
evaluator is MTG-181's, ported unchanged in behavior to main's
`eval`/`sim` modules. The anchor is main's strengthened `HeuristicPlayer`
(639ab7770). This is the anchor MTG-181's endpoint addendum uses. Its bytes moved to
`manabot/sim/heuristic.py` and changed only in an import, which changes its
source digest and so the derived per-game player seeds: deals and seats are
paired with MTG-181's addendum; policy sampling draws are not.

Local CPU, replay-checked `play_cell`, stochastic policy, 600-second and
10,000-command game limits, ten group workers. Score: win 1, draw ½, loss 0, over
valid games only; invalid games are counted and shown, never replaced; a failed
group is not replayed. 95% Wilson intervals describe game noise for one
checkpoint, not seed variance.

**Checkpoints:** MTG-186's exports at updates 0, 200, 400, 600 and the endpoint,
each on the full grid. For matched-update curves on the training matchup,
MTG-181's update-200/400/600 exports (all that exist: 18101 at 200/400/600,
18102 and 18103 at 200/400) play only the two UR/GW cells of the same schedule
(UR vs heuristic GW and GW vs heuristic UR, 100 games each). MTG-181's endpoints
on the full grid come from its addendum (`experiments/data/mtg181/new-heuristic`).
Nothing is selected.

## Predictions, fixed before allocation

MTG-181 seed 18101 at u616 against this heuristic, from 4,150 of its 5,000
addendum games already played: about 0.58 overall, 0.90 as UR vs heuristic GW,
0.46 as GW vs heuristic UR.

- Training: 616 updates in 4–6.5 hours of training, 4–8M learner decisions per
  active hour; learner decisions equal environment decisions; no illegal
  action, non-finite value or replay mismatch; game-error rate far below 10%.
  Inclusive cost $2.50–$5.
- Training matchup: at the endpoint the two UR/GW cells together score at
  least as high as MTG-181's mean at matched updates. The GW-vs-UR cell gains
  most (endpoint 0.50–0.75); the UR-vs-GW cell stays high (0.85–1.0). A
  difference under about 0.10 on 100 games is within game noise and one seed.
- Off the training decks: overall score below MTG-181's endpoints, 0.30–0.50.
  Rows UR and GW stay above every other row. Other rows still gain over the
  specialist's initialization (partial transfer through shared cards and basic
  play), by less than MTG-181's rows.

Refutation, a failed run and no useful effect are reportable outcomes. Kill
criteria are the job's own: failed numerics gate, non-finite values, illegal
offered action, identity mismatch, game-error rate above 10%, or exhausted
time/cost allowance. Stop rather than tune or substitute.
