## Concept review — 2026-10-28 (accepted outcome; proposed implementation)

Accepted: Jack Heart commissioned exploration of full TLA support, with shared rules-faithful semantics and a readable plan. This authorizes research, not full implementation. Research evidence and proposed implementation remain distinct from accepted product direction.

Jack Heart accepted option 1a on 2026-10-28: the eventual outcome admits any valid user-supplied TLA deck through the common human/bot engine. He explicitly requires decklist-file input, which can be its own PR and proceed alongside card work. A named format contract and supported file dialect remain to specify; a deckbuilder, draft/sealed UI and multiplayer are separate outcomes.

Jack also directed at most eight total TLA PRs, staged on their prerequisites, with at most two or three concurrent card groups; automated builds must come to Jack for review. The proposed allocation below uses seven TLA PRs plus one decklist PR, keeping even the combined total at eight. The exact grouping is proposed for review, not accepted merely because these constraints are accepted.

Concrete mismatch: `SemanticPack::compile_content_pack` already lowers reviewed IR into native `ContentPack`; `select_authored_runtime` and `CuratedPack.player_config` require authored deck setups. Thus adding definitions does not by itself admit new deck combinations. Do not describe the IR lowerer as a second live rules engine. Reference definitions and the diagnostic interpreter are evidence to assess against rules, not automatically authoritative or automatically removable.

Proposed usage-doc excerpt: “Choose a versioned TLA pool and submit two deck lists under a named format. Admission reports unsupported cards or invalid setup before a game starts. Both players and bots receive the same engine choices. Reconnecting restores the actual pending choice; replay uses the original world and content identity.” This describes a target, not a command available today. During incremental delivery, partial content is labeled partial; complete support must include interactions between admitted cards.

Proposed maintainer-guidance excerpt: “Add a rules-faithful semantic definition and lower it into the shared native runtime. Verify behavior independently of registry parity; then qualify it for the versioned pool. Do not implement game semantics in Academy or bypass admission because a card name is registered.” Existing command, privacy and replay guidance remains binding.

Model/API consequences of the accepted outcome (implementation design proposed): separate immutable pool membership (content identity) from deck/setup admission (deck lists, format, constraints) and from the game record (world/content/setup identity plus committed Commands). Derive readiness from evidence; keep the research ledger out of runtime authority. An illustrative new shared `admit_setup(pool, format, decks)` boundary would return an admitted setup or explicit issues; naming and placement remain design work. Preserve existing `execute_semantic_command_json` revision/offer binding and receipt/observation result, and Academy `resume` semantics. Freeze historical authored setups; qualify a new pool without rewriting earlier evidence. No separate human-only legality implementation.

Counterexamples/next proof: a novel combination of individually qualified cards must use the same compiled content and complete actions for human and bot; if a cross-card interaction fails, individually passing card tests are insufficient. An unsupported face must reject setup explicitly, without substitution or starting a partial game. Disconnect during a pending choice and resubmit a stale Command: committed costs remain, stale mutation is rejected, and the restored pending decision survives. An older replay stays bound to its original world/pool. These are proposed acceptance scenarios, not verified results of this review.

Review artifact: `scratch/concept-review.html`. Option 1a is accepted; do not ask it again. Proposed PR boundaries and sequencing are in `full-tla.md`. Jack reviews each built PR; this exploration has not launched implementation.
