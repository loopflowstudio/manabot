# Learner admission and backpressure — bounded research

The prior turn added exact request timing and a second independent tape. This
slice addresses the remaining learner/age gap without selecting a distributed
learning algorithm. No remote allocation, training or production change.

Observed source boundaries: `execution.py:1270–1355` collects a frozen behavior
batch, then updates, advances its iteration and EMA. `ataraxos.py:211–310` builds
central targets/filtering and takes one optimizer step per retained timestep.
`docs/distributed-rl.md:19–24,99–108,627–638` explicitly leaves nonzero-lag admission
unselected. A full512row batch with16streams has32 timestep groups; with64streams
it has8. The real L4 runs observed96 versus24 optimizer steps across three updates,
so rows/s alone cannot extrapolate learning to arbitrary global batch shapes.

Calibration: the reference64stream x8step L4 TrainingRun from l4-003. Use its
summed collection/learning seconds divided by its three iterations; preserve the
source hash and full shape/counters. Constant service is a short-run sensitivity
input, not a service distribution, steady state or a new CUDA measurement.

A pure scheduling model will count complete512row batches, policy versions,
production-start timestamps, queue waits and one learner owner. Strict zero-lag
admission discards batches made with an older version before learning starts.
Serial production is the current-compatible control. Block/drop-full producer
modes are hypothetical transport experiments; neither authorizes stale learning.
Blocking a producer retains its completed packet outside the bounded queue;
report that occupancy separately. Count completed learning, stale/full discards,
waiting/held/active/in-production units and enforce conservation. Do not call
admitted rows useful strategic learning or scale independent optimizers linearly.

Before execution: reuse the remaining legacy240second cap (232.202used), one
<=5second pure-Python supervisor invocation. Check closed-form serial throughput,
zero stale admission, full/drop/block accounting and deterministic replay. Sweep
8/64/4096synthetic producers, two queue capacities and block/drop modes at declared
offered-rate multiples. This is an independent admission stress model; it is not
yet coupled to request-level inference, network, GPU sharing or real policy changes.
Prediction: bounding the queue alone does not bound all completed packets when
producers block; speculative zero-lag production can waste work even below nominal
learner capacity. Preserve counterexamples rather than relax the admission rule.

## Findings

`scratch/learner-flow-probe.py` completed17cases and closed-form serial/partial
service, fractional-clock, zero-lag, block/drop, conservation and deterministic
replay checks. Three bounded invocations consumed0.703commandseconds, no GPU cost.
Final report:`accelerator-probes/learner-flow-clock.json`; earlier reports and
source snapshots remain. A1e-12second horizon roundoff rule fixes a fractional
completion landing a few ulps beyond the cutoff; policy versions remain exact.

Calibration is0.395460s collection and0.250497s learning per512row 8x64 batch,
from three real L4 iterations. This gives a nominal learning-only capacity of
2,044rows/s, not whole-run throughput or a portable capacity for another shape.
The serial control completed20iterations with no stale/full discards; its
learner duty fraction was38.78%, accounting for collection time separately.

At4096complete-batch producers, queue capacity1 and block-on-full, the model
retained up to511completed packets:509held by producers, one queued, one active.
Drop-on-full kept that peak at2but discarded510packets for overflow. Both modes
learned only2of8193produced batches; the other packets were stale or full-discarded.
This horizon includes startup/drain behavior, not a steady-state throughput claim.
At8producers offering only half nominal learner capacity,17batches were produced,
2learned and15discarded as stale. Every admitted batch had version lag0. Wall-clock
age and version lag are distinct: admitted samples can be old in seconds while
still current-version. Explicit retained counts include work pending at cutoff.

Interpretation: queue length and generated rows/s are insufficient optimization
gates. Count producer-held results, admission/discarded work, unique rows,
optimizer exposures, batch shape and policy version. A bounded queue cannot make
stale learning valid. Candidate next controls include a global batch lease, different zero-lag quotas,
or an explicitly tested alternative learning rule; none is selected here. The next model needs a measured global-cohort service point and
integration with inference sharing. Independent optimizer owners cannot be summed
as though they were one consistent learner. These are scheduling findings, not
new learned-policy results or a production distributed implementation.
